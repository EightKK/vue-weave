# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added

- TreeGrid: opt-in column resizing, header sorting and header filtering, as in GanttChart. `columnsResizable` (with `minColumnWidth`, `maxColumnWidth` and `validateColumnResize`) adds a separator to the tree column header and each column header that proposes `column-resize` (`TimelineColumnResize`; `timelineLabelColumnId` names the tree column, accepted through `treeColumnWidth`) by pointer drag or arrow keys, or `column-resize-rejected`. `sortableColumns` and `filterableColumns` add sort buttons (`aria-sort` on the header of the first rule) and filter dialogs that propose `update:sort` and `update:filters` (`v-model:sort`, `v-model:filters`) from the controlled `sort` and `filters`. New optional `TreeGridLabels`: `resizeColumn`, `columnResizeRejected`, `sortBy`, `sortedAscending`, `sortedDescending`, `filterBy`, `filtered`, `filterContains`, `filterValues` and `clearFilter`. Without the new props the grid renders and behaves as before.
- Core: `projectTreeGridRows(input, { sort, filters })` applies those proposals while keeping the hierarchy (siblings sorted within their parent, filtered rows with all their ancestors, the sparse cells of the kept rows), and `treeGridCellText(index, rowId, columnId, options?)` gives the text the grid shows in a cell. The "Tree grid" example adds a cost breakdown with these header controls.
- TaskBoard, EventCalendar and TreeGrid expose `exportSvg(options?)` and `exportPng(options?)` like NetworkGraph and GanttChart (`TaskBoardExportOptions`, `EventCalendarExportOptions`, `TreeGridExportOptions` and their `Png` variants), with the theme colours and font in effect on the component. The board exports every column, lane and shown card; the calendar the shown day, week or month view with the whole time grid; the grid every displayed line and column, not only the rendered window. Core: `renderTaskBoardSvg`, `renderEventCalendarSvg` (a view it does not export throws a `RangeError`) and `renderTreeGridSvg` (with optional `sort` and `filters` applied by `projectTreeGridRows`, `rowRange` and `columnRange`) build the same documents without a DOM.

### Changed

- `levelResources` is much faster on long horizons: placing a task now reads remaining capacity and free working time only as far as that task's placement needs, instead of from its earliest start to the end of the horizon. In the benchmark of `docs/performance.md`, 3,000 tasks went from about 1 s to under 0.2 s and 10,000 tasks from about 13 s to under 1 s. Results are unchanged: the same dates, leveled tasks, delays, overloads, unresolved tasks, traces and errors.

### Fixed

- NetworkGraph and `renderGraphSvg` centre the text of plain rectangle nodes on the node height, as they already did for outline shapes. Before, the title and detail lines sat at fixed offsets made for the default height of 58, so a shorter node (for example `nodeHeight: 30`) drew its text across or below its bottom edge, and a taller node drew it near the top. Output at height 58 is unchanged.
- Documentation site examples: the option bars wrap whole controls onto a new line instead of breaking checkbox and button labels word by word, the Japanese linked-views and scenario graphs widen their nodes to fit the longer detail text, and the construction showcase keeps its milestone table and critical-path dates inside the panel on narrow screens.

## 1.6.0 - 2026-11-23

Adds an opt-in `idle-escape` to NetworkGraph and GanttChart and the core helper `taskViewBindings`, so applications clear a selection on Escape and link task views without bookkeeping of their own. The production demo is now one linked, saving app, the scheduler is checked by property tests and a brute-force oracle, and a leveling fault they found is fixed.

### Added

- NetworkGraph and GanttChart report an opt-in `idle-escape` (`@idle-escape` in templates, `.once` included; declared as the listener prop `onIdleEscape` and delivered as a component event) with the `KeyboardEvent` when Escape is pressed in them and cancels nothing of their own: no gesture, armed connection or open cell editor (so no `gesture-end` or `edit-cancel` for it; an Escape anywhere in the component, on its zoom buttons or minimap too, cancels those first), not in a text field, not handled by the label editor, a filter dialog or a spreadsheet range, and not part of an input method composition. Applications clear their selection on it as on `background-click`, instead of counting `gesture-start` and `gesture-end` or watching `edit-cancel` around a wrapper element. The examples and showcases use it and no longer carry that bookkeeping.
- Core: `taskViewBindings({ tasks, assignments?, dependencies?, inactiveTaskIds?, taskKind?, dependencyKind? })` returns the `ViewBinding[]` of a plan whose views show tasks and dependencies under their own IDs: each task bound to its graph node, timeline bar and row and, per assignment, the load ranges in which it uses its resource; each dependency to its graph edge and timeline dependency. It takes a schedule's tasks and the assignments of `calculateResourceLoad` and binds load through `assignmentWorkPeriods`, so a load segment selects exactly the tasks `calculateResourceLoad` counts in it: pauses, calendar breaks and the work outside a partial assignment's `workOffset` and `workDuration` select nothing. Spread it with bindings of your own into `useLinkedSelection`.

### Changed

- The production demo (`/demo/` and the production showcase) is now one linked, saving app built only on existing public APIs. The planning and lot flow views share one `useLinkedSelection`: an operation selected in the graph, the Gantt chart or the load chart is the matching lot step in the lot flow, and a step selected there is the operation in planning, across tab switches. The approval workflow and the schedule sheet have unrelated data and keep their own selection. Every tab saves through one app-wide `useEditTransaction` against the mock server, with a save bar that shows saving, rejected, conflicting and unanswered saves, app-wide undo and redo, and a choice of the server's next answer. Adopting a plan proposal closes that proposal when its save is accepted, directly or after an unanswered save is checked with the server, and leaves a proposal opened during the save open; its list of rescheduled operations is derived from the plan it is compared with, so it follows a save that is accepted or refused meanwhile. While a save's result is unknown, nothing else is saved, undone or redone until it is checked, and the save bar of the demos (`examples/components/SaveStatus.vue`) offers to check it or to load the latest version but no longer to dismiss it, which could leave the app showing an older version than the server's. The mock server validates the other planner's change like any save, and the schedule sheet shows why a loaded sheet does not schedule and stays editable instead of failing. The multi-view guide says how to tie such state to its save, that saves, undo and redo must wait while a result is unknown (`useEditTransaction` refuses a new save only while one is in flight), and that documents from `refresh` or the server skip the adapter's `validate`.
- The scheduling guide now states how ALAP subtasks treat links from their summary: an FS or FF link limits their finish, and an SS or SF link limits the start of each subtask whose earliest start is the summary's earliest start, as in the backward pass. The behaviour is unchanged.

### Fixed

- `scheduleTasks` no longer throws `RangeError: Maximum call stack size exceeded` for a summary task with more than about 100,000 links that limit its subtasks, and NetworkGraph with `edgeLabelPlacement` no longer throws it when a move changes that many nodes: both passed an input-sized list to a single `push(...list)` call, which now is a loop.
- `levelResources` no longer misses capacity shortages before time 0 for tasks with a partial assignment (`workOffset` or `workDuration`) when the horizon starts before 0. Such a task could stay overloaded without being listed in `unresolvedTaskIds`, or keep a start where a resource it needs is closed instead of moving.
- GanttChart: in right-to-left charts, Safari (WebKit) drew dependency lag labels far from their dependency, beyond the start of the time axis. Each label was turned back inside the mirrored dependency layer by a CSS `scaleX(-1)` about its own centre, which needs `transform-box: fill-box` on SVG text, and WebKit flipped it about the layer's origin instead. The label now turns back through its own `transform` attribute (a flip about its anchor, with a start anchor written as `text-anchor="end"`), so it covers the same span in every engine. The stylesheet no longer sets `transform` on `.vw-gantt-lag`.
- GanttChart spreadsheet: Ctrl+X (Cmd+X) in Safari copied the selected cells but did not clear them. WebKit runs the copy event of `document.execCommand('copy')` from a cell without a text selection yet returns false, and the chart cleared only on `true`. It now also clears when its own copy handler wrote the selection during that call.
- GanttChart: Ctrl+wheel and Meta+wheel zoom (`wheelZoom`) no longer drifts away from the pointer during a trackpad pinch in Safari. WebKit keeps whole-pixel scroll positions, and each of the many small wheel events started from the rounded position. While the scroller still reports the position the last wheel zoom set, at the same zoom, the next event starts from the requested (fractional) position; where scroll positions keep the fraction, nothing changes.
- EventCalendar: the server-rendered week title no longer causes a hydration mismatch where the browser's ICU formats the range with another pattern than Node.js (Firefox, `ja`: "2026年11月9日～15日" against "2026/11/09～2026/11/15"). The title element carries Vue's `data-allow-mismatch="text"`, so hydration takes the browser's text, as it already displayed, without logging a mismatch.

## 1.5.0 - 2026-11-23

Adds an opt-in `background-click` event to NetworkGraph, GanttChart, TaskBoard and EventCalendar, so applications can clear a controlled selection when empty space is clicked, and the examples and showcases now clear their selection on such a click or Escape. Glyph buttons inside the components (zoom, row toggles, add and remove buttons) are drawn as SVG icons and stay centered whatever font an application gives buttons. Every change is backward compatible.

### Added

- NetworkGraph, GanttChart, TaskBoard and EventCalendar emit an opt-in `background-click` when empty space is clicked without dragging, so applications can clear a controlled selection (the components keep none of their own). NetworkGraph reports the point in graph coordinates; GanttChart a `TimelineBackgroundClick` `{ time, rowId? }` for an empty part of the time area (not in rows where the click creates a bar); TaskBoard a `TaskBoardBackgroundClick` `{ columnId?, laneId? }`; EventCalendar an `EventCalendarBackgroundClick` `{ time, allDay, resourceId? }` when the click proposes no `create`. Nothing else changes for applications that do not listen.
- The examples and showcases clear their selection when you click empty space in a graph, chart, board or calendar, or press Escape there (an Escape that cancels a drag keeps the selection).

### Fixed

- The zoom-in and zoom-out buttons of NetworkGraph and GanttChart, the GanttChart row toggle and add button, the TreeGrid row actions (add below, add child, delete) and the PropertyInspector remove button draw their symbols as SVG icons, so they stay centered when an application restyles button fonts. Their accessible names are unchanged. The markup of these buttons changes from a text character to an `svg` element (`vw-zoom-icon`, `vw-row-toggle-icon`, `vw-gantt-create-icon`, `vw-tree-grid-action-icon`, `vw-inspector-remove-icon`); the icons use `currentColor`.
- The construction showcase no longer applies its button style to the chart's own row toggles, which overlapped neighbouring rows.

## 1.4.0 - 2026-11-22

Adds `TreeGrid`, an editable tree grid and matrix, and core helpers for relation-aware graph walks, keyed graph expansion, structure diffs, row-tree projections and time bucket tables, plus entity-initiated linked selection for views of your own. The examples, showcases and API reference move to a new bilingual documentation site (English and Japanese) at https://eightkk.github.io/vue-weave/. Every change is backward compatible.

### Added

- `TreeGrid`, an opt-in controlled tree grid and matrix: a row tree against columns, with cells from column accessors (`value(row)`) or a sparse `cells` list. Cells keep a value (including 0), known none, unknown, not applicable and not loaded apart (`missingCell`, cell `status`). Rows and columns are virtualized together, the focused cell stays rendered and focused outside the window, and the ARIA treegrid keys reach every cell. Children can load on demand (`childStatus`, `expand-request`, a status line). Expansion and the active cell are controlled (`v-model:expanded-ids`, `v-model:active-cell`). `row-activate` and `cell-activate` report entities, for linking through `useLinkedSelection().selectEntities` and `matchTreeGridEntities`. Opt-in editing proposes `cell-edit` with typed editors (text, number, select, checkbox, date and time; IME-safe), `spreadsheet` proposes one `sheet-edit` per paste, fill (Ctrl+D or the fill handle), clear or cut, and `rowsEditable` proposes `row-create`, `row-delete` and `row-move` (keyboard and pointer). Each proposal has a synchronous validator, `-rejected` events and `edit-cancel`. Core helpers `createTreeGridIndex`, `flattenTreeGrid`, `applyTreeGridEdits`, `insertTreeGridRow`, `deleteTreeGridRows` and the window and key functions, the `--vw-tree-grid-*` theme variables, right-to-left, forced-colors and print support, server rendering, and a "Tree grid" demo.
- `useLinkedSelection` gains `selectEntities(entities, { source }?)` and `selectionSource`, so views without a `LinkedViewName` (an application's own table or list) can start a linked selection through entity references. The selection has no owner, every view only highlights the bound targets, `selectedEntities` keeps the given entities in input order (unbound ones included, listed in `unknown`), and repeating the same entities from the same source is a no-op that does not call `onSelect`. `ViewTarget` and `LinkedViewName` are unchanged. The linked planning demo has an external task list.
- Core: `diffStructure(before, after, { key, fields?, parentKey?, equals? })` compares two versions of a flat or tree-shaped keyed collection, such as two revisions of a bill of materials, a WBS or a product hierarchy. It returns `added` keys (in after order), `removed` keys and `changed` items with each changed field's `before` and `after` values, and, with `parentKey`, the `moved` items whose parent differs (in before order). `key` and `parentKey` take a property name or a function. The default fields are the own properties of both items, without the key and parent properties; values compare with `Object.is` unless you pass `equals`. Keys follow the shared ID rule (`Duplicate item ID: a (before)`); unknown parents and cycles throw an Error. It uses loops only and never changes its inputs. New types: `StructureDiffOptions`, `StructureKeyAccessor`, `StructureDiff`, `StructureChange`, `StructureFieldChange` and `StructureMove`.
- Core: `projectTimelineRows(input, state)` is the row part of `projectTimelineView`, for applications that show rows in their own tree table. It applies the search and column filters (matches keep their ancestors), sorts siblings stably by sort rules or columns, and drops collapsed IDs that name no row. It returns `rows`, `matchedRowIds`, `wbs`, `collapsedIds` and the row diagnostics (`unknown-row`, `unknown-column`, `unknown-sort-rule`). `bars` are optional: with them, the search reads bar labels and columns receive row summaries. `projectTimelineView` now uses it, and its output is unchanged. New types: `TimelineRowsInput`, `TimelineRowViewState` and `TimelineRowsProjection`.
- `traverseGraph` takes opt-in `edgeFilter(edge, direction)` to follow only some relations (it is told the walk direction), `maxNodes` to cap the result nearest first (starts count and are always kept; with `'both'` the two walks advance hop by hop together and a node counts once), and `paths`. With any of them the result adds `truncated` (`{ nodeId, direction, reason: 'max-hops' | 'max-nodes' }[]`, where a limit stopped a walk); with `paths: true` it adds `via` (one `Map` per walk from each reached node to its first step `{ edgeId, previousId }`) and `multiplyReachedIds`. Without the new options results are unchanged. New types: `GraphWalkDirection`, `GraphTraversalTruncation`, `GraphTraversalStep`, `GraphTraversalVia`, `GraphTracedPath`.
- `graphTracePath(result, nodeId, { direction }?)` rebuilds the nodes and edges from a start to a node from a `traverseGraph` result made with `paths: true`, using the fewest-hops path within one walk.
- `resolveTimeRangeTargets` and `useLinkedSelection` `timeRange.traversal` accept the new `edgeFilter` and `maxNodes`.
- Keyed graph expansion: `beginGraphExpansion`, `cancelGraphExpansion`, `mergeGraphExpansionPage` and `graphExpansionView` take an optional `{ key }` (`GraphExpansionKeyOptions`), for example `'forward'`, `'backward'` or a relation name, kept in the new optional state fields `expandedIdsByKey` and `pendingIdsByKey`. `graphExpansionView(state, { key })` returns the frontier of that key. An unkeyed merge still marks a node expanded for every key, and calls without a key behave as before.
- Time bucket tables in the core: `createTimeBucketColumns` turns sorted, non-overlapping half-open periods (gaps allowed; `calendarBands` days or weeks in an IANA zone work as they are) into column descriptors `{ id, label, width, start, end }` with deterministic IDs, and `aggregateTimeBuckets` aggregates time-stamped instants or intervals (placed by start, overlap or prorated share) into one sparse cell `{ rowId, columnId, value, status, count }` per row and bucket, with the rules `sum`, `count`, `min`, `max`, `first`, `last`, `ratio` (numerator and denominator, never averaged percentages), `precomputed` (server values as-is) or a custom reducer, per row. Buckets outside an optional `loaded` coverage (ranges, or a function such as `windowedCoverage`) are `unloaded` with a null value instead of 0, loaded buckets without items are `loaded-empty`, and `carryForward` opts `last` rows into running balances. `timeBucketIndex` and `timeBucketIndexRange` find buckets by binary search, so a period table can follow GanttChart's `TimelineVisibleWindow`. See [Time bucket tables](docs/api.md#time-bucket-tables).
- Core `treeGridCellsFromTimeBuckets(cells)` and `treeGridCellContentFromTimeBucket(cell, extra?)` turn `aggregateTimeBuckets` cells into TreeGrid cells: a loaded value stays a value (a `sum` of nothing is 0), a loaded bucket without a value is `none`, and buckets that are not loaded keep their `unloaded`, `loading` or `failed` status instead of showing 0.

### Fixed

- A server-rendered `EventCalendar` hydrates without a mismatch: Node.js formats the week title (`Intl.DateTimeFormat#formatRange`) with thin spaces around the dash where browsers write plain spaces. Formatted dates and times in the library (the `EventCalendar` title and event names, `intlTimeFormatter`, `calendarZoomPresets` and locale-aware `calendarBands` labels) now write thin, narrow no-break and no-break spaces (U+2009, U+202F, U+00A0) as plain spaces, so the server and the browser render the same text. The default English output is unchanged apart from those spaces.
- Documentation: types and placeholders with angle brackets in `docs/` (such as `ReadonlySet<string>` or `Edit <column> for <row>`) are in code spans, so GitHub and npm show them instead of dropping them as HTML tags.

### Demo

- New "Tree grid" demo: about 3,000 products against 30 columns with editing, spreadsheet operations, row structure editing, a lazily loaded group, undo and redo, linked to a NetworkGraph through `selectEntities`.
- New "Period table" demo: a TreeGrid of sites, items and measures against day or week columns (`calendarBands`, `createTimeBucketColumns`, `aggregateTimeBuckets` with `sum`, `last` + `carryForward`, `min` and `ratio`). Days that are not loaded show their load state, never 0, and a mid-week shortage is marked inside a positive week. Columns follow a GanttChart's visible window (`timeBucketIndexRange`), demand cells are editable with undo, and selecting a cell highlights the orders that feed it.
- The example gallery (`gallery.html`) is retired: every example and showcase is on the documentation site, and `gallery.html` forwards old links (`?demo=<id>`, `#<id>`) to the matching page, or to the example index. The production demo links to the example index.
- The **Calendar views** showcase marks Ascension Day (Berlin) and Corpus Christi (Lisbon) instead of a Labour Day closure, whose header label was drawn over the Golden Week label.
- A new design gallery showcase, **BOM editor**: a multi-level bill of materials with shared subassemblies, edited in `TreeGrid` and linked to an item graph, with where-used walks (`traverseGraph` with `edgeFilter`, `maxNodes` and `graphTracePath`), a revision comparison with `diffStructure`, search with `projectTimelineRows` and a quantity rollup.

### Documentation

- A documentation website built with VitePress, in English with a Japanese version under `/ja/`: getting started, an examples index by category with a page per example (live demo, source code tabs, related API), a showcase gallery, and an API reference whose pages are built from `docs/*.md` (overviews in both languages, detail tables in English) plus a type reference generated with TypeDoc for both entry points. Every example and showcase shows its own text and the components' labels in Japanese on Japanese pages. The site replaces the demo homepage and the example gallery; the production planning app stays at `/vue-weave/demo/`.
- README.md and README.ja.md are shorter and point to the website.

### Development

- `npm run build:demo` builds the site (with the generated type reference) into `preview/` and checks every internal link and anchor; `npm run dev` starts the site's dev server and `npm run dev:demo` the standalone demo. The site build (about 2,000 pages with the type reference in both languages) needs more than Node's default heap on machines with little memory, so `build:demo` and `dev` run VitePress with a 6 GiB heap limit. `scripts/serve.mjs`, `npm start`, `Start.cmd`, the Playwright tests and the offline demo archive serve the site under `/vue-weave/`. New development dependencies: `vitepress` (2.0 prerelease, which uses Vite 8), `typedoc`, `typedoc-plugin-markdown` and `typedoc-vitepress-theme`, with an npm `overrides` entry for TypeDoc's `minimatch`. The package contents and runtime dependencies are unchanged.

## 1.3.0 - 2026-11-22

Adds recurring events, automatic edge label placement, GanttChart header sorting and filtering, PropertyInspector assignment and dependency fields, TaskBoard collapse, filters and WIP minimums, and an opt-in CSV formula escape. It also fixes the findings of a full review of the components, the core and the interchange (including imports that a small hostile file could stall for seconds), and refactors large parts of GanttChart, NetworkGraph and the core without changing their results. A few changes are not strictly backward compatible: they are listed under "Breaking changes" and marked **Behaviour change** under "Fixed"; read them before upgrading.

### Breaking changes

- `resourceLoadHistogram` now throws a `RangeError`, not an `Error`, for load segments that overlap within a resource, like its other checks of times, load and capacity (an unknown resource still throws an `Error`). GanttChart reports overlapping `loadSegments` through `validation-error` with the same `RangeError`. Who is affected: code that tells this failure apart by its class, for example `error.constructor === Error` or `!(error instanceof RangeError)`. What to do: check for `RangeError`; the message is unchanged.

### Added

- Recurring events (RFC 5545 RRULE), opt-in: new core functions `parseRecurrenceRule`, `formatRecurrenceRule`, `expandRecurrence`, `expandRecurringEvents` and `applyRecurringEdit` with the types `RecurrenceFrequency`, `RecurrenceWeekday`, `RecurrenceRule`, `RecurrenceZoneOptions`, `CalendarRecurrence`, `RecurrenceSeries`, `RecurrenceOccurrence`, `RecurrenceExpandOptions`, `RecurringEditScope`, `RecurringEditTarget`, `RecurringEditChange` and `RecurringEditOptions`. DAILY, WEEKLY, MONTHLY and YEARLY rules with INTERVAL, COUNT, UNTIL, BYDAY (with ordinals), BYMONTHDAY, BYMONTH, BYSETPOS and WKST, plus EXDATE and RDATE, expand in an IANA time zone or fixed offset and keep their local time across daylight-saving changes; sub-daily frequencies, BYHOUR, BYMINUTE, BYSECOND, BYYEARDAY and BYWEEKNO are rejected. `CalendarEvent` gains the optional `recurrence`, `seriesId` and `recurrenceId`, `EventCalendarEdit` the optional `occurrence`, and `EventCalendarLabels` the optional `recurring`; EventCalendar names occurrences "…, Recurring" and marks them with `vw-is-recurring`, while events without these fields render as before. `applyRecurringEdit` applies an edit to one occurrence (override or EXDATE), to it and the following ones (split series) or to the whole series. `fromICalendar` takes `recurrence: 'series'` (parsed rule, EXDATEs, RDATEs and RECURRENCE-ID overrides) or `recurrence: { range }` (expanded occurrences, new type `ICalendarExpandOptions`); the default is unchanged. `toICalendar` writes RRULE, EXDATE and RDATE for events with `recurrence`, with TZID times and a VTIMEZONE for an IANA zone. The "Event calendar" demo has a weekly sync with a choice of edit scope.
- `TaskBoard` (opt-in, controlled): collapsible columns and lanes (`collapsible`, `v-model:collapsed-column-ids`, `v-model:collapsed-lane-ids`; collapsed areas render a narrow strip or only a header, are skipped by the keyboard and are not drop targets), a `filter` prop with "3 of 7" column counts (moves keep indices that count hidden cards, so `applyBoardCardMove` on the full array is right), and `TaskBoardColumn.wipMin` marking columns under a minimum (`vw-is-under-minimum`). `groupBoardCards` takes `{ filter }`; `TaskBoardCell` gains `indices` and `total`, `TaskBoardColumnStatus` gains `total`, `wipMin` and `underMinimum` (these new fields are required, so code that builds `TaskBoardCell` or `TaskBoardColumnStatus` objects by hand, for example in test doubles, must add them; values from `groupBoardCards` already have them), and `TaskBoardLabels` gains `wipMin`, `underMinimum`, `collapse` and `expand`. New CSS variables `--vw-board-wip-under`, `--vw-board-collapsed-width` and `--vw-board-collapsed-fill`. The "Task board" demo shows them. Without the new props nothing changes.
- NetworkGraph `edgeLabelPlacement` prop (opt-in, default `'midpoint'`, which keeps the current output): `'avoid'` or an options object moves edge labels along and beside their routes, away from node boxes, other labels and other edges where there is space. It runs after all routing, `avoidNodes` included; drag and resize previews place the labels of the changed edges and those near the changed nodes, and exportSvg and exportPng draw the placed labels. The diagram styles demo has a "Place labels clear" toggle.
- Core `placeGraphEdgeLabels(layout, edges, options)` with `GraphEdgeLabelPlacementOptions` (`fontSize`, `glyphWidth`, `labelSize`, `labelText`, `gap`, `margin`, `samples`, `edgeIds`, `obstacles`), `GraphEdgeLabelSize` and `GraphEdgeLabelPlacement`: a deterministic greedy label placement that returns a new layout and keeps the `edgeLabels` anchor meaning (centre and baseline of the text).
- GanttChart header sorting and filtering, opt-in: `sortableColumns` (true or column IDs, `timelineLabelColumnId` for the row label column) adds a sort button to each header ("Sort by Finish") that cycles that column's rule through ascending, descending and none, keeping the other rules, and proposes the sort through `update:sort`; the button name carries the controlled `sort` ("Sort by Finish (ascending)"). `filterableColumns` adds a filter button that opens a small dialog with a text filter (and a value list for a `select` field), proposing `update:filters` for the controlled `filters` prop; Escape closes it and returns focus. The chart never reorders or filters rows; apply the proposals with `useTimelineView` (new `setFilters`). New `GanttLabels` entries `sortBy`, `sortedAscending`, `sortedDescending`, `filterBy`, `filtered`, `filterContains`, `filterValues` and `clearFilter`. Without the new props the markup is unchanged.
- Timeline views: `TimelineViewState.filters` (`TimelineViewFilter`: `columnId`, optional `text` and `values`), optional so states saved without filters still parse; `projectTimelineView` keeps the ancestors of filtered rows and sorts or filters by row label for `timelineLabelColumnId`. New core helpers `filterTimelineRows(rows, predicate)` (matches with their ancestors) and `nextTimelineSort(sort, columnId)`.
- PropertyInspector list fields, opt-in: `assignments` (resource select, units, add and remove) and `dependencies` (predecessor, FS/SS/FF/SF type, lag, add and remove) tables in `fields`, with accessible names such as "Units for Crane" and "Remove Crane". Each edit proposes the whole list through the new `list-change` event, checked by the new core `validateInspectorList` and a synchronous `validateListChange` (`list-change-rejected`, reason shown inline). New types `InspectorListFieldKind`, `InspectorOption`, `InspectorAssignment`, `InspectorDependency`, `InspectorAssignmentsField`, `InspectorDependenciesField`, `InspectorListField`, `InspectorListChange` and `InspectorListChangeValidator`, and optional `PropertyInspectorLabels` entries for the table. `change` and `InspectorChange` are unchanged.
- `toCsv(table, { escapeFormulas: true })` (opt-in, new type `CsvOptions`) writes text cells that start with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return with a leading apostrophe, so spreadsheets do not run them as formulas. Number cells are unchanged, and without the option the output is the same as before.
- `GraphEdgeLabelPlacementOptions.edgeWidth(edge)`, opt-in: the drawn width of wide edges such as flow bands, so a label keeps its `gap` from the outer side of its band and other labels stay off the band. NetworkGraph passes the `edgeBands` widths when `edgeLabelPlacement` is on. `labelSize` and `edgeWidth` may return `null` as well as nothing.

### Fixed

- `fromDrawio` and `fromDrawioAsync` read deeply nested containers in linear time (20,000 levels took about 36 seconds, now under half a second) and collect the label cells of edges in linear time; label cells now count against `maxItems` like nodes, edges and groups, so a file with more than `maxItems` items including its label cells now throws a RangeError.
- `toDrawio` writes a node label that has a line break also as `vwLabel` in the style, so `fromDrawio` reads it back whole instead of splitting it into label and detail. Files without `vwLabel` are read as before.
- `timelineFromCsvTable` builds WBS ancestors and the row order without quadratic work (a 20,000-level code in about 40 KB took about 10 seconds) and throws a RangeError for a WBS code of more than 1,000 levels.
- `toMsProjectXml` writes carriage returns in project, task, calendar and resource names as `&#13;`, so they survive a round trip instead of being read as line feeds.

## 1.0.0 - 2026-11-21

This is the first stable release. From here on the package follows SemVer as defined in [Compatibility and versioning](docs/api.md#compatibility-and-versioning), and it is published on npm as `@eightkk/vue-weave` (`npm install @eightkk/vue-weave`). The changes below fix data-correctness and compatibility problems found in the pre-release review; the ones that change results or types are listed under "Breaking changes", each with who is affected and what to do.

### Breaking changes

- The package name is now `@eightkk/vue-weave` (was `@eightkk/vue-weave`), because npm does not accept the unscoped name. The entries are `@eightkk/vue-weave`, `@eightkk/vue-weave/core`, `@eightkk/vue-weave/style.css` and `@eightkk/vue-weave/package.json`, and the release tarball is `eightkk-vue-weave-<version>.tgz`. Who is affected: everyone who installed an earlier tarball. What to do: install `@eightkk/vue-weave`, remove `@eightkk/vue-weave`, and replace `'@eightkk/vue-weave'` with `'@eightkk/vue-weave'` in imports (also `/core` and `/style.css`). The exports, types and behaviour of each entry are otherwise as described below.
- `fromICalendar` now resolves a `TZID` local time that a daylight-saving change skips as RFC 5545 section 3.3.5 requires: with the UTC offset in effect before the change. `DTSTART;TZID=America/New_York:20070311T023000` is now 07:30 UTC (local 03:30) instead of 07:00 UTC. This applies to DTSTART, DTEND and the end of a day or week `DURATION`. A repeated local time still takes its first occurrence. Who is affected: anyone who imports `.ics` files with `TZID` times that fall in a skipped hour; re-importing such a file gives a start or end up to one gap length (usually one hour) later than 0.6.0 did, so events imported with 0.6 and re-imported with 1.0 can differ. What to do: re-import those files if you stored 0.6.0 results and need the RFC times. `buildWorkPeriods`, calendar bands and editing keep moving a skipped time to the change itself. `toICalendar` writes UTC and is unaffected.
- `comparePlans` now throws when a given successful `result` does not match the task set of its `input`: an active task is missing or listed twice, or `inactiveTaskIds` does not list exactly the inactive tasks (subtasks of an inactive summary included). A result calculated before a task was added, removed or (de)activated used to be compared as a complete plan, with the new task `unscheduled` and a stale finish. Who is affected: callers who pass a precomputed `result` that does not exactly match the input (stored, cached or hand-built results). What to do: recalculate the result whenever its input changes, or omit `result` and let `comparePlans` calculate it. Only task sets are checked; a result for an earlier version of the same tasks (a changed duration, say) is still not detected, so keep a shared revision if you store results separately from their input.
- `parseFieldValue` with a `select` spec that has `multiple: true` now always returns an array, also for a single entry (`'a'` → `['a']`, was `'a'`). GanttChart's built-in field input therefore proposes arrays for such columns. Who is affected: code reading `field-edit` `after` or `parseFieldValue` results for multiple selects. What to do: expect an array.
- `formatFieldValue` for a `time` field with `timeZone` now appends the offset to a wall time that occurs twice (for example `2026-11-01 01:30 -05:00` in `America/New_York`), so the text parses back to the same instant. Who is affected: code that displays, compares or parses this text itself. What to do: accept the `±HH:MM` suffix.
- GanttChart row heading text (`vw-row-content`) is now a focusable `role="button"`, named by its content, with one Tab stop for the heading column (see "Added"). Who is affected: tests that look up buttons with loose or regular-expression names (for example `getByRole('button', { name: /^Task/ })`), which can now also match a heading. What to do: use exact names, or exclude the heading.
- `setWindowedView`, `retryWindowedData` and `windowedCoverage` now throw `RangeError` for a window whose chunk indices are not safe integers (finite times too far from `origin` for `chunkSize`), instead of looping without end. `setWindowedView` also throws `RangeError` when the chunk boundaries `origin + k·chunkSize` of the view are not distinct finite numbers, and `windowedCoverage` throws when the window's cell count exceeds `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`. `useWindowedData` reports these views through `error`. Who is affected: only views with extreme ranges or origins, which previously hung. What to do: use a larger `chunkSize` or an `origin` closer to the window.
- Types only: `ScheduleGraph` (returned by `scheduleToGraph`) now types `nodes` as `NetworkNode<ScheduleGraphNodeData>[]` and `edges` as `GraphEdge<ScheduleGraphEdgeData>[]`, so node and edge `data` (critical flag, early dates, lag) can be read without casts. The arrays still assign to `NetworkNode[]` and `GraphEdge[]`. Who is affected: code that pushes items with other data into `graph.nodes` or `graph.edges`. What to do: copy the arrays into your own `NetworkNode[]`/`GraphEdge[]` first.

### Added

- GanttChart rows can be activated from the keyboard. Enter or Space on a row heading emits `target-activate` with source `keyboard`. ArrowUp, ArrowDown, Home and End move between headings, through a single roving tab stop. Controls inside a `row` slot keep their own keys.

### Fixed

- The type declarations of `useTimelineView` (`state`) and `useEditTransaction` (`historyReset`) no longer use the two-argument `ShallowRef<T, T>`, which Vue 3.5.0 does not declare, so strict TypeScript projects on the minimum supported Vue (`^3.5.0`) no longer fail with TS2707.
- GanttChart cell inputs (plain and typed) and the Escape handling around `cell-editor` leave Enter and Escape of an input method composition (`isComposing` or keyCode 229) to the input method. Confirming a Japanese conversion no longer commits the half-typed text or resets the input; a later Enter commits. The built-in inputs also keep the typed text, composed or not, as a draft across re-renders (new bars, row height and other prop changes used to reset it to the source value); a commit, Escape or new source text for that cell ends the draft.
- Committing unedited text in a typed field cell, or `commit(value)` with the current value, ends the edit with no `field-edit` and no validation. Values whose text did not parse back no longer produce changes.
- `parseFieldValue` reads `select` labels as well as values, so the text `formatFieldValue` shows can be committed again. Duplicate labels are rejected as ambiguous with a `choice` error and formatted as values. In multiple selects, an entry that contains a comma or a double quote, or starts or ends with whitespace, is written in double quotes with inner quotes doubled (as in CSV), and the parser splits only at unquoted commas, so every valid selection parses back to the same array (`Cut, weld` is two entries, `"Cut, weld"` one). A choice with an empty label is written by its value in a single select. A choice set in which some choice has no text that names it (its label and its value both read as other choices, or values such as `1` and `'1'` with the same text) makes `formatFieldValue` and `parseFieldValue` throw an `Error`, instead of reading a valid value back as another one; give that choice a label of its own.
- Time fields with `timeZone` round-trip repeated wall times: format then parse gives back the same instant. Typed text without an offset still resolves to the earlier occurrence.
- `useAsyncGraphLayout` now holds the results and failures of the frame being computed apart from its reuse cache. With `cacheSize: 0` or `1` a frame no longer re-requests the same layouts forever without being shown. A failed request is no longer silently repeated when its other scope succeeds, so `display.source === 'layout'` no longer appears next to a stale `error`. A failed frame is requested again when the input changes. `cacheSize` now only sets how many layouts are kept for reuse by later inputs.
- `graphLayoutWorkerAdapter` rejects a response whose `requestId` and `inputKey` match but whose `scope` differs. It removes its `message` and abort listeners however a request ends, including when posting the request to the Worker throws (the request then rejects with that error) and when the cancel message cannot be posted.
- `setWindowedView` no longer walks every chunk of the time range for a view with an empty `rowIds`; such a view requests nothing.
- `windowedCoverage` no longer counts unloaded cells one at a time. It computes `total` from the chunk and row counts and counts only the recorded cells, so checking a whole plan over a wide range stays cheap.
- `useWindowedData` no longer throws when the first read of `condition` fails (a getter that throws, or a value that is not a string), including during server rendering. That failure, and a `view` or `condition` getter that throws later, which the watcher used to swallow, is reported through `error`, and the view is requested again once the value is valid.
- `parseWeaveSnapshot` and `restoreWeaveSnapshot` of timeline snapshots, and `projectTimelineView` without `laneCount`, no longer throw `RangeError: Maximum call stack size exceeded` for timelines with very many bars (for example 200,000). GanttChart's `fitToBars`, NetworkGraph's collapsed-group moves, group frames and actuals diagnostics append large lists with loops for the same reason.

## 0.6.0 - 2026-11-19

This is a minor release because of the changes under "Breaking changes": error messages of input checks are unified, an ID must be a non-empty string everywhere, `layoutGridGraph` has a new default `columnGap`, and `graphRectsIntersect` no longer counts touching rectangles. Each entry below says who is affected and what to change.

### Breaking changes

- Graph layouts: every built-in layout (layered, tree, radial, circular, grid, concentric, swimlane, force, flow and incremental) now reports an invalid numeric option in one format that names the option, the rule and the value: `RangeError('<option> must be <rule> (got <value>)')`, with the rules `a finite number > 0`, `a finite number ≥ 0`, `a finite number`, `an integer ≥ 1`, `a finite number in [0, 1]` and `a safe integer`. Before, the texts varied, for example `columnGap must be finite and positive (spacing may be zero)`, `rowGap must be finite and nonnegative (node sizes positive)`, `nodeWidth and nodeHeight must be finite and positive` or `crossingSweeps must be a positive integer`; now `columnGap must be a finite number ≥ 0 (got -1)` and `crossingSweeps must be an integer ≥ 1 (got 0)`. The error class (`RangeError`) and the accepted values are unchanged. What to do: update code or tests that match these messages.
- `layoutGridGraph`: the default `columnGap` is 50 (was 40), the default of every other layout, so a grid without an explicit `columnGap` is 10 units wider per column gap. What to do: pass `columnGap: 40` to keep the earlier geometry.
- `layoutLayeredGraph`: same-rank edges (inside a cycle) and self-loops turn `min(24, max(6, columnGap / 2))` units past the box, as in the tree and swimlane layouts (was `min(24, columnGap / 2)`). Only a `columnGap` below 12 changes the drawing: the turn stays 6 units out instead of shrinking towards the box, and the layout can grow by up to 6 units along the flow when such an edge leaves the last rank. What to do: nothing, unless you compare paths of such layouts exactly.
- `graphRectsIntersect(a, b)`: rectangles that only touch, along an edge or at a corner, no longer intersect; the test now uses open intervals (`a.x < b.x + b.width && b.x < a.x + a.width`, and the same for y), compared exactly. A zero-size rectangle intersects a rectangle whose interior it passes through, not one whose border it lies on. `cullGraphElements` (and so `cullOffscreen`) still keeps elements whose bounds touch the view, because their strokes can still draw into it. What to do: if you need touching rectangles to count, test `a.x <= b.x + b.width && b.x <= a.x + a.width` (and y) yourself.
- `GraphTreeLayoutOptions`, `GraphSwimlaneLayoutOptions` and `GraphIncrementalLayoutOptions` now extend `GraphLayoutOptions` instead of declaring `direction`, `nodeWidth`, `nodeHeight`, `columnGap`, `rowGap` and `padding` themselves. Names, types and optionality are unchanged, so every existing object literal still type-checks; code that augments one of these interfaces with a conflicting declaration of a shared field must now match `GraphLayoutOptions`.
- One ID rule and one set of error texts for the planning, timeline and data core (see [Validation and errors](docs/api.md#validation-and-errors)). An ID must be a non-empty string, unique within its collection; any other value is invalid and throws an `Error`. The interchange, windowed data and snapshot checks already used the strict rule; only their texts change.

It returns a `GraphSwimlaneLayout` (a `GraphLayout` plus `lanes`: band, header, `index` and `nodeIds` per lane). Types `GraphSwimlaneSpec`, `GraphSwimlaneBand`, `GraphSwimlane`, `GraphSwimlaneLayout` and `GraphSwimlaneLayoutOptions`.
- Quantity flow (Sankey) projection: `layoutFlowGraph(nodes, edges, { quantity, unit, displayUnit, direction, nodeThickness, nodeGap, rankGap, scale, ... })` returns a `GraphFlowLayout`, a `GraphLayout` for the `layout` prop plus `nodes` (`GraphFlowNode`: box, rank, `inflow`, `outflow`, `difference`, `value` and the reference totals), `bands` (`GraphFlowBand`: edge ID, quantity, unit, `thickness = quantity × scale` on one scale for the whole picture, drawn `width`, source and target sides and offsets, centre-line path and a label such as `Cut → Inspect: 60 pcs`) and `excluded` (`GraphFlowExcludedEdge`). #### Scheduling and resources

- Summary tasks in the scheduler: `ScheduleTask.parentId` makes a task a subtask, and a task that other tasks name as parent is a summary task whose dates are the span of its subtasks. FS and SS links into a summary bind the start of every subtask (FF and SF links into a summary throw an Error, as Microsoft Project does not allow them); links from a summary use its rolled-up start or finish. A summary's `startNoEarlierThan` and `finishNoLaterThan` bind its subtasks; `fixedStart`, `startNoLaterThan`, `finishNoEarlierThan`, `manual` and `mode: 'alap'` on a summary throw an Error. Results mark summaries with `summary: true`, give them the span of their subtasks' latest dates, the smallest float of their subtasks and a critical flag when any subtask is critical, and give subtasks their `parentId`. Unknown parents, parent cycles and links between a summary and its own subtask throw an Error that names the tasks; a dependency cycle through a summary's roll-up is a `cycle` failure. `propagateEdit` refuses to edit a summary, `rescheduleFromStatusDate` frees started subtasks from the start bounds of their summaries, `levelResources` levels the subtasks and gives summaries no load, and `buildPlacementInput` keeps what binds the placed subtasks of an unplaced summary: they move to their nearest placed ancestor and take over the `startNoEarlierThan` and `finishNoLaterThan` of the unplaced summaries they skip, stay inactive below an inactive one, and links into an unplaced summary still bind its placed leaf subtasks (an `unplaced-predecessor` diagnostic when the link comes from an unplaced task). A placed summary that is active but has no placed active leaf subtask is an `unplaced-subtasks` diagnostic (not `ok`) and is left out like an unplaced summary, instead of being scheduled with its own duration. `buildPlacementInput` checks the summary rules on the whole input and throws the scheduler's Error for a rule broken by unplaced tasks too. Plans without `parentId` are scheduled exactly as before. See [Summary tasks](docs/scheduling.md#summary-tasks).
- `PlacementResult.retargetedDependencies` (`PlacementRetargetedDependency`: `id`, `dependencyId`, `summaryId`, `taskId`), present only when not empty: the links of a placement input that replace a link from a placed task into an unplaced summary, one per placed leaf subtask, so that `criticalDependencyIds` and traces map back to the input link.
- `buildPlacementInput` diagnostic `{ code: 'unplaced-subtasks', taskId }` (`PlacementSummaryDiagnostic`) for a placed summary task without a placed active subtask; it comes before the `unplaced-predecessor` entries (now also exported as `PlacementPredecessorDiagnostic`) and makes `ok` false.
- Time-varying resource capacity, opt-in: `ResourceCapacity.capacityPeriods` (`ResourceCapacityPeriod`: `start`, `end`, `capacity`) replaces a resource's `capacity` over fixed half-open intervals; outside them `capacity` applies, and outside the availability `periods` the capacity stays 0. Overlapping, empty or inverted periods and invalid capacities throw a RangeError that names the resource. `calculateResourceLoad` splits its segments where the capacity changes and measures overload against the capacity valid at each time, so GanttChart's capacity line and `renderTimelineSvg` follow it with no new prop; `levelResources` reserves against the same capacity, so leveling and the load calculation agree. Planning snapshots validate the field.
- Assignments for part of a task, opt-in: `ResourceAssignment.workOffset` and `workDuration` limit an assignment to part of the task's working time, measured along its scheduled `work` segments, so the part moves with the task and skips breaks, pauses and splits. A part beyond the work is clipped. `calculateResourceLoad` and `levelResources` count the resource only during the part, `projectResourceTimeline` still shows the whole task on its row and `proposeResourceAssignment` keeps the part on a replacement. New `assignmentWorkPeriods(work, assignment)` returns the part's elapsed periods. Input without the new fields gives the same results as before. See [Compare demand with capacity](docs/scheduling.md#compare-demand-with-capacity).
- Rescheduling from recorded actuals, opt-in: `rescheduleFromActuals(input, statusDate, actuals)` takes recorded actual intervals per task (`TaskActuals`: half-open `intervals`, optional `remainingWork` and `finished`), keeps them exactly as recorded, also on non-working time, and schedules only the remaining work from the status date (or the last actual end, if later) on the task's calendar, links and bounds. The default remaining work is the duration minus the elapsed length of the actuals, at least 0. Started tasks drop their start bounds and incoming FS and SS links and keep FF and SF links, as in `rescheduleFromStatusDate`; finished tasks end at their last actual end. The `RescheduleFromActualsResult` has the forecast `schedule`, the original `plan`, per-task `ActualsTask` entries with separate `actual` and `remaining` work, and diagnostics (`ActualsDiagnostic`: `actual-overlap`, `actual-after-status-date`, `actual-outside-calendar`, `actual-contradiction`, followed by those of the schedule); an infeasible forecast is returned with the actuals kept. Summary, manual and inactive tasks are supported. `rescheduleFromStatusDate` is unchanged. `actualsTimelineBars(result, options?)` (`ActualsTimelineBarOptions`) turns the result into GanttChart bars that show the original plan as a baseline, the recorded actuals as a solid `<label> · actual` bar and the remaining forecast as an outline `<label> · forecast` bar on one row.
- Plan scenario comparison: `comparePlans(current, candidate)` compares two `PlanScenario`s (a `scheduleTasks` input with optional resources and assignments, optional precomputed `result` and `load`, `revision`, `baseRevision` and `partial`) and returns a `PlanComparison` with separate `input` differences (settings, tasks, dependencies, calendars, resources and assignments, with the changed field names) and `result` differences (schedule state per side, final completion, per-task date shifts and critical changes, tasks edited versus moved as a consequence, critical dependencies, `deadline`, `manual-conflict` and `leveling-unresolved` violations gained and lost, and cumulative overload per resource). Cycles and infeasible plans are reported, not thrown; partial plans make the comparison incomplete and a moved base revision marks it stale. `planComparisonTargets` turns compared tasks into view and load targets for the highlight props, and `planBaselines` turns a schedule into a named baseline set. `PlanComparisonTask.deadline` is an optional due time that the scheduler ignores. New types `PlanComparisonTask`, `PlanComparisonInput`, `PlanScenario`, `PlanScheduleState`, `PlanTaskSide`, `PlanFieldChange`, `PlanCollectionDiff`, `PlanAssignmentChange`, `PlanAssignmentDiff`, `PlanInputDiff`, `PlanViolation`, `PlanTaskComparison`, `PlanResourceOverload`, `PlanResultDiff`, `PlanComparison`, `PlanComparisonTargetOptions` and `PlanComparisonTargets`.

## 0.5.0 - 2026-11-17

This is a minor release because of the two type changes under "Breaking changes": TypeScript code that lists every value of `ScheduleReasonKind` or `PlanningDiagnosticCode` needs a new case. Code that runs today behaves the same, since the new values only appear once a plan uses manual tasks. Everything else is additive and opt-in: without the new fields, props and options, results, server markup, routes, exports and accessible names are the same as in 0.4.0.

### Breaking changes

- `ScheduleReasonKind` gains `'manual'`, used in traces (`{ explain: true }`) for the dates of manual tasks.
- `PlanningDiagnosticCode` gains `'manual-conflict'`. `toPlanningDiagnostics` lists each entry of `manualConflicts` as a warning with the task and the link, before unresolved tasks and overloads; with `explain`, its `reasonIds` name the task's `manual` reason. A result without manual conflicts gives the same list as before.

A `switch` or `Record` over every value of either union needs an entry for the new value.

- The demo hides its graph Undo and Redo buttons in the timeline editing example, where they changed the hidden graph and timeline instead of the shown timeline.
- Restore Vue type checking and declaration generation with TypeScript 6.0.x; hold Dependabot's TypeScript 7+ updates pending compatibility with `vue-tsc`.
- Remove the source-only stylesheet import from generated declarations so strict `noUncheckedSideEffectImports` consumers can import the components.
- Report missing row parents and hierarchy cycles through Gantt `validation-error`, render the empty state and recover on valid replacement data.
- Keep empty graph layouts measurable with zero padding in both LR and TB directions.
- Resolve ESM declarations in strict TypeScript NodeNext consumers, including Vue component declarations.
- Report invalid Gantt input through `validation-error(error)` and render the empty state in production. Development builds surface the rethrown error. Validate updated `pixelsPerUnit` and `scale` props.
- Keep a focused Gantt bar rendered outside the visible window and reveal it after an accepted keyboard edit.
- Reuse long-edge routing lanes so graph headroom depends on overlapping edges.
- Expose zoom-control group names and localize graph rejection and edge-selection labels.
- `levelResources` keeps a split task at the start it reserved. Before, an end bound (`finishNoEarlierThan`, FF or SF) could pull the start earlier during the final recalculation, placing work in resource downtime or on capacity taken by another task while `unresolvedTaskIds` stayed empty. Split tasks now always get a `startNoEarlierThan` in `leveledTasks`, and a task whose final work differs from its reserved slices is reported in `unresolvedTaskIds`.
- `graphPathBounds` reads numbers with `e`/`E` exponents (`1e-7` was treated as a command and threw), tracks the reflected control point of S and T, and bounds arcs exactly after SVG radius correction and rotation. Culling and SVG/PNG export no longer drop or clip such edges. Malformed path data, including stray characters and a command without arguments, now throws `Invalid SVG path data`.
- Reconnect handles sit at the drawn edge ends after saved positions swap two nodes or a custom layout picks its own sides. `graphEdgeEndpoint` now takes unnamed endpoints from the drawn path of an edge with an `id`; new `graphPathEndpoints(path)` returns a path's first and last points.
- Fit to view shows the whole graph even when that needs a scale below 0.1. Zoom, pinch, `setViewport` and `focusNode` accept scales down to that fit scale, so a fitted viewport can be restored. The automatic initial view keeps its 0.1 floor.
- Gantt grid lines are drawn at every tick position (new `vw-gantt-grid-line` and `vw-gantt-tick-line` elements) instead of a repeating CSS background, so they match the tick labels and the SVG export on non-linear `scale`s and in right-to-left charts. The tick step is chosen from the narrowest visible tick gap instead of the first interval; linear scales choose the same step as before.

### Editing behavior

- Gantt controlled props are watched by reference. Pass fresh arrays and objects; in-place mutations do not trigger validation or cancel active edits.
- Editable Gantt bars emit `select` once per click, on release, including after a drag.
- Gantt pointer edits scroll automatically near horizontal viewport edges. Set `autoScroll` to `false` to disable this behavior.
